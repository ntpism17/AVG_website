## How Net Billing and Net Metering Differ

Both systems are mechanisms for buying back surplus electricity from rooftop solar, but their underlying calculation principles differ fundamentally.

**Net Billing** calculates electricity a user "buys" from the utility and electricity they "sell" back into the grid as two separate line items. The surplus sold back is converted into a "monetary value" at a state-announced buy-back rate — it is not a direct offset of electricity units. This system requires a digital meter that measures two-way electricity flow (import/export measured separately), and gives the government room to adjust the buy-back rate according to policy over time.

**Net Metering** directly offsets electricity units on a 1:1 basis — surplus electricity produced is later offset against electricity consumed, valued at the retail electricity rate. There is no cash payback, and technically it can use a conventional (mechanical) meter without needing to upgrade to a digital one.

## The System Thailand Actually Uses Is Net Billing, Not Net Metering

This needs to be stated clearly: **Thailand uses Net Billing as its actual, currently enforced main system — not Net Metering** — per a resolution of the National Energy Policy Council (NEPC), which sets the surplus electricity buy-back rate at **2.20 baht per unit**, compared to the retail electricity rate general users pay of roughly 3.99 baht per unit.

The Department of Alternative Energy Development and Efficiency (DEDE) has explained three main reasons Net Metering has not yet been adopted:

1. **Regulatory constraints** — relevant regulations and laws (including the tax system) don't yet have a framework supporting a direct Net Metering offset.
2. **Technical constraints** — a grid designed for centralized generation still needs infrastructure upgrades (smart meters, smart grid, transformers) to support large volumes of electricity flowing back from rooftop solar simultaneously.
3. **Fairness to electricity users** — the strongest concern is that Net Metering could shift costs onto general electricity users who haven't installed solar, particularly lower-income households who can't afford to invest in solar panels but would bear the increased system cost through their electricity bill.

The policy framework therefore weights self-consumption of self-generated electricity over commercial sell-back, while maintaining fairness in cost burden across all electricity-user groups.

## Why the Term "Net Metering" Still Keeps Coming Up in Thai Solar Conversations

Even though Net Billing is the system actually enforced, the term Net Metering still appears frequently in Thai solar-energy articles and conversations. The reason is that it is a **demand from civil society and residential solar advocacy groups**, who view the 2.20 baht/unit Net Billing buy-back rate as too low relative to the retail rate, and want the government to switch to Net Metering so households with solar installed receive a higher return value. In short, Net Metering in the Thai context is **something being demanded**, not an enforced system — conflating the two terms without distinction is a common error, and a point where a lot of content on the Thai internet gets it wrong without realizing it.

## Why This Distinction Matters for Metering and Billing Work

For anyone working on electricity metering systems, meter communication system design, or billing in the Thai solar market, mistakenly believing Thailand uses Net Metering can lead to designing a system to the wrong spec from the start — because Net Billing requires a meter that accurately measures and records incoming and outgoing electricity separately, with a communication system that supports calculating monetary value at the buy-back rate, a technical requirement entirely different from Net Metering. Using the wrong term in a proposal document or project spec is therefore not just a matter of terminology — it affects credibility with clients and regulators who already know this subject well.

This level of precision — clearly distinguishing which system Thailand actually uses, and measuring two-way electricity correctly against the buy-back rate — is something Advanced Grid's AMR and billing systems treat as fundamental to every project.
