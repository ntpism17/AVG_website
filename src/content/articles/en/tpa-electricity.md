## What Is TPA (Electricity)?

TPA (Third Party Access — the right to use and connect to the power grid granted to a third party) in Thailand's electricity market is a mechanism that lets private renewable-energy producers "wheel" electricity through a utility's transmission and distribution systems directly to another private buyer — without first selling that power into the utility's system and buying it back. This mechanism is the foundation that makes a Direct PPA (Direct Power Purchase Agreement) between two private parties possible over the existing grid.

The policy starting point was the National Energy Policy Council (NEPC) resolution of 25 June 2024, which approved the framework for a 2,000-megawatt (MW) Direct PPA pilot project, targeting primarily electricity users who need large volumes of clean energy, such as data centers and foreign investors. To trade electricity this way, both buyer and seller must apply for TPA service from the grid operator, under the oversight of the Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC).

## Electricity TPA ≠ Natural-Gas TPA Code — Two Entirely Different Things

People searching for "TPA" in Thailand often confuse it with the ERC's older TPA Code, which is entirely unrelated to the electricity TPA discussed above:

- **TPA Code (natural gas)** — regulations the ERC has published since 2015, covering third-party access to the natural-gas pipeline network and LNG terminals operated by PTT, intended to reduce monopoly control and let new gas traders compete. PTT's onshore TPA Code was approved 30 March 2015; the LNG-terminal TPA Code has a first edition dated 30 March 2015, a second edition dated 21 October 2015, and a most recent third edition dated 31 March 2020 — this matter **has nothing to do with electricity**.
- **TPA (electricity)** — a new mechanism that only entered its service-charge rate-setting process in 2025, specifically to support the Direct PPA pilot project. It shares the same abbreviation because the underlying principle — "opening existing network infrastructure to third-party use" — is the same, but it applies to a different type of infrastructure (power transmission/distribution lines, rather than gas pipelines).

If a search leads to the ERC's TPA Code page (erc.or.th/th/tpa) and the content discusses natural gas or LNG, that page is about a different matter than the electricity TPA this article covers.

## The TPA Service Charge: Still Being Determined

The ERC held a public hearing on the draft "service charge for third-party use of and connection to the power grid (TPA) for the renewable-energy Direct PPA pilot project" through 10 November 2025. The charge structure has 5 main components:

1. **Wheeling Charge** — the fee for using the transmission/distribution lines to carry electricity from seller to buyer.
2. **Connection Charge**
3. **System Security Charge**
4. **Imbalance Charge**
5. **Policy Expenses**

At the time of writing, the exact baht-per-unit rate is still going through the public-hearing process, and no final confirmed rate has been announced that this article can cite [pending — verify against https://www.energynewscenter.com/กกพ-เปิดรับฟังความเห็น-6/].

## Why This Matters for Anyone Who Has to Meter and Bill Electricity

When electricity flows through the grid under a TPA agreement between Direct PPA contracting parties, accurate metering and a billing system consistent with all 5 components of the charge structure above become an indispensable baseline requirement for every party in this new electricity-trading chain.
