## What IPP, SPP and VSPP Mean

IPP, SPP and VSPP are Thailand's classification of private power producers by size and selling model — terms used in official documents and power purchase agreements, with criteria issued by regulators such as the ERC and the Energy Policy and Planning Office (EPPO).

- **IPP (Independent Power Producer)** — a large private producer selling into the grid to EGAT.
- **SPP (Small Power Producer)** — a smaller private producer covering both cogeneration and renewables.
- **VSPP (Very Small Power Producer)** — a very small private producer, such as rooftop solar, selling to a distribution utility (PEA/MEA).

The capacity thresholds used to divide the groups follow the latest announcements. Generally, a VSPP is no larger than 10 MW, an SPP falls around 10–90 MW, and an IPP is larger than 90 MW [pending — verify exact MW thresholds against EPPO/ERC announcements].

## How They Differ in Practice

The difference is not only size but also counterparty and the structure of power purchase. An IPP signs a long-term power purchase agreement directly with EGAT. An SPP may sell to EGAT or a distribution utility depending on the terms. A VSPP usually sells to PEA/MEA at a feed-in rate based on fuel type and installed capacity.

Licensing burden, technical requirements and reporting formats also differ. Project developers therefore need to know which group they fall into, to prepare documents and systems correctly from the start.

## Why Private Power Producers Need Accurate Billing

Whichever group they belong to, private power producers earn revenue from the units the meter actually measures. Recording and calculating those units to match the meter data sent to the utility is work that cannot be wrong — because a mismatch affects both payment and regulatory scrutiny.

A billing system that pulls real meter data and calculates and reconciles automatically is therefore essential infrastructure for private power producers, especially as the number of sites and billing cycles grows.
