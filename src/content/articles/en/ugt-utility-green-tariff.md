## What Is UGT?

**UGT (Utility Green Tariff)** is an electricity rate for power generated from renewable sources such as hydro, solar, wind, and biomass. Buyers on this tariff receive a **Renewable Energy Certificate (REC)** alongside their power purchase contract, serving as evidence of clean-energy use that meets international standards.

The Thai government introduced UGT to support international environmental policy commitments and to help attract foreign investment where clean-energy conditions are set by an overseas parent company or business partner. UGT is structured into two tiers: UGT1 and UGT2.

**UGT1** is unspecified-source green electricity, generated and operated by 7 hydropower plants under EGAT (the Electricity Generating Authority of Thailand), on a short 1-year contract, priced at the standard electricity rate plus a 0.0594 baht/unit premium (excluding VAT). MEA (the Metropolitan Electricity Authority) opened its first registration round from 2–24 January 2025. PEA (the Provincial Electricity Authority) is Thailand's other main utility, but the specific rollout details for UGT within PEA's service area could not be confirmed from the sources available for this article [pending — verify against https://www.pea.co.th/news/infographic/928].

## Why UGT Matters to Corporate Electricity Buyers and ESG Teams

For organizations that must report renewable-energy use under standards like RE100 or supply-chain requirements from an overseas parent company, UGT is a mechanism that lets them buy clean electricity bundled with a REC directly through the utility, without investing in their own generation system. Large industrial and data center buyers with continuous clean-energy needs are the primary target group for this tariff — particularly at the UGT2 level, which lets buyers specify their electricity source more precisely.

The main regulator of this mechanism is the **ERC (Energy Regulatory Commission)**, which sets the pricing framework and terms, together with **EGAT**, which acts as both a power producer and an advisor on UGT2's pricing structure.

## How Does UGT2 Differ From UGT (UGT1)?

**UGT2** differs from UGT1 in that it lets electricity buyers **specify the source of their electricity**, drawing capacity from new renewable power projects approved under the **2022–2030 Feed-in Tariff (FiT)** scheme — not from the same 7 existing hydropower plants as UGT1 — and carries a minimum contract term of **10 years**, rather than UGT1's 1-year contract.

UGT2's pricing structure is split into two groups based on when each plant entered commercial operation: Group A at 4.5622 baht/unit (plants commissioned 2025–2027) and Group B at 4.5475 baht/unit (plants commissioned 2028–2030). Both groups draw from fuel-cost-free energy sources. UGT2's primary target group is Type 4–5 industrial electricity users and data centers that need to demonstrate renewable-energy use to meet ESG conditions.

At various points when this was reported, the ERC gave several different estimated timelines for UGT2's launch — some sources placed it around mid-2025, others around an announcement in October 2025 — and UGT2 remains linked to the **2,000-megawatt Direct PPA pilot project**, for which the ERC held a public hearing on criteria and pricing structure from 3–10 October 2025, ahead of a targeted official procurement announcement in December 2025. The detailed technical mechanisms separating UGT2 from Direct PPA — such as the hourly method for matching generation to consumption — could not be confirmed with clear detail from the sources available [pending].

Whether UGT1, UGT2, or another form of clean-electricity trading, the credibility of any such tariff depends on production and consumption data that is actually measured by meters, confirming that the volume of clean energy delivered matches what the contract specifies.
