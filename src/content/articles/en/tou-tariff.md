## What Is TOU

**TOU (Time of Use)** is an electricity tariff that charges different prices depending on when power is used. Periods of high system-wide demand cost more, and periods of low demand cost less. The principle encourages users to shift consumption to times when the grid has spare capacity, reducing the overall investment burden on the power system.

For large electricity users and industrial plants, TOU is the most familiar tariff structure, because the distribution utilities (PEA/MEA) apply it to high-volume customers. Understanding which rate applies to each unit consumed is therefore fundamental to checking a bill and planning energy use.

## How On-Peak and Off-Peak Differ

A TOU tariff divides time into at least two main periods:

- **On-Peak** — when system-wide demand is high, typically weekday working hours; the most expensive per unit.
- **Off-Peak** — when demand is low, such as nights, weekends and public holidays; cheaper per unit.

The exact hours for each period depend on the tariff type (such as the time-of-use rate) and each utility's published schedule, so they should be read from the rate shown on that site's electricity invoice.

## The Ft Adjustment and Customer Categories

On top of the time-based base tariff, Thai electricity bills also carry the **Ft (variable electricity charge)**, an adjustment that reflects changing fuel and other costs. It is revised every four months under the regulator's announcement, so the total bill can move even when the base tariff is unchanged.

Utilities also divide customers into several categories (residential, small business, medium business, large business), each with a different tariff structure. Large users and factories are therefore usually placed on a TOU tariff that charges by time of use, making billing more complex than a typical residential bill.

## What This Means for Factories and Solar Developers

For solar project developers and factory operators, calculating electricity charges correctly under TOU is the heart of credibility, because every unit actually consumed carries a different price depending on the time period, and the Ft charge plus the share of self-generated solar must also be folded into the calculation. A billing system that pulls real meter data and applies these rates automatically goes a long way toward reducing errors and billing disputes.
