## What Is Direct PPA

**Direct PPA (direct power trading between a producer and a buyer)** is a mechanism that lets industrial electricity users — particularly Data Centers and manufacturing plants — buy clean electricity directly from a power producer over the transmission grid, instead of buying entirely through the conventional utility channel. This differs from a regular PPA in the Thai market, which is a contract between a power producer and one of the utilities.

The programme is one of the Ministry of Energy's Quick Big Win policies, driven by the Minister of Energy (Atthaphon Krit-Phibun), with the **ERC (Energy Regulatory Commission)** overseeing the programme's implementation and assessing its impact on other sectors, alongside the Ministry of Energy.

## Why This Matters Now

Direct PPA is scheduled to begin operation in January 2026, with the government estimating the programme's investment value at **65 billion baht** and expecting it to create over **3,000 jobs**.

In the pilot phase, the ERC is opening a total of **2,000 megawatts** of direct private electricity purchasing, with Data Centers that need renewable energy to meet requirements from overseas parent companies as the primary target group. Ahead of the launch date, the ERC laid out the following preparation timeline:

- **October 3–10, 2025** — Public consultation on criteria, technical standards, and price structure
- **October 15, 2025** — Consideration by the ERC board
- **November 2025** — Submission to the National Energy Policy Council for consideration
- **December 2025** — Official electricity-procurement announcement

Direct PPA also connects to the **UGT2** mechanism, which is solar and wind electricity sold to large industrial electricity users, drawing capacity from Feed-in Tariff (FiT) projects from 2022–2030 totaling roughly 4,852.26 MW, split into Group A (power plants commercially operating 2025–2027, priced at 4.5622 baht/unit, roughly 2,386 MW) and Group B (operating 2028–2030, priced at 4.5475 baht/unit, roughly 2,466 MW). Both groups are 10-year contracts for Type 4 and 5 electricity users.

At the policy level, Thailand's new draft Power Development Plan (PDP), which frames the outlook through 2050 and targets roughly 60% clean energy of total generation capacity, includes Direct PPA as a liberalized electricity-market mechanism letting industry buy clean power directly over the transmission grid, alongside strict ERC oversight of electricity licenses for Data Centers to prevent impacts on grid stability and other electricity users — since national Data Center electricity demand overall is estimated as high as roughly 30,000 MW. This draft PDP is expected to be finalized around August–September 2026.

## What This Means for Solar Developers and IPPs

For solar project developers and IPPs planning to sell power to industrial groups or Data Centers, the emergence of Direct PPA opens a sales channel beyond conventional utility contracts — but it comes with technical-standard and pricing conditions the ERC is still in the process of setting. Developers should therefore closely track the published criteria and price framework, to assess project feasibility in time for the official electricity-procurement opening.

Whether the contract structure is Direct PPA or another form of Private PPA, the accuracy of metering and billing between contracting parties remains central — because correct meter data is the foundation of billing and of verifying the actual clean energy volume delivered under the contract.
